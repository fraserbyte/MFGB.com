Historical Timeline

Milestones of the Kabinenroller

From an invalid carriage in Rosenheim to a two-stroke icon on the roads of Britain — trace the journey of Fritz Fend and the machines that bear the Messerschmitt name.

Messerschmitt microcar historical timeline

  1. 1948

    Fritz Fend creates the Fend Flitzer

    In Rosenheim, technical designer Fritz Fend develops the Fend Flitzer, a single-seat invalid carriage with an aircraft-style control column — the direct ancestor of the Messerschmitt Kabinenroller.

  2. 1952

    Partnership with Willy Messerschmitt

    Fend establishes a partnership with Professor Willy Messerschmitt in Regensburg. With the aircraft factory turned to peacetime production, the Fend designs are developed under the Messerschmitt name.

  3. 1953

    Launch of the KR175 Kabinenroller

    The KR175 enters production — a compact cabin scooter with tandem seating, a 175cc Fichtel & Sachs two-stroke engine, and a side-hinged acrylic canopy inspired by the cockpit of a fighter.

  4. 1955

    Debut of the updated KR200

    The KR200 arrives with an enlarged 191cc Fichtel & Sachs engine and an improved, independent front suspension — delivering higher output and a markedly better ride over its predecessor.

  5. 1958

    Production peak & the ME 200

    Production peaks as demand for affordable mobility soars. From this era comes our featured exhibit, ME 200 — a 1958 KR200 in original specification, its patina preserved by the Foundation.

  6. Today

    The Foundation today

    The Messerschmitt Foundation of Great Britain carries on Fritz Fend's legacy across the country — preserving surviving Kabinenroller, verifying provenance and chassis numbers against our archive, and helping owners keep their two-strokes on the road through restoration guidance, events, and the community registry.
